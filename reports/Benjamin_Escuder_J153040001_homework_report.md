# Homework Report

I ran my tools on the Reddit stock sentiment dataset (newdataset/Reddit-stock-sentiment.csv, text column "text", label column "label"). The question I looked at was whether the words used in a Reddit post relate to the sentiment label of that post. I hit API rate limits twice during the run, so the chat was restarted two times. This is why the result IDs are spread out (the first run's last reported result is binarize_8, the second run ends at patterns_22, and the last one goes from load_dataset_23 to reduce_29). Each restart reloaded the same file with the same settings, and the numbers came out the same.

**Loading and cleaning.** load_dataset_1 loaded 847 posts: 423 neutral (0.0), 315 negative (-1.0), 109 positive (1.0). The classes are not balanced, and positive is the smallest. check_duplicates_2 found 23 duplicate texts covering 34 rows, and I dropped all copies, so 847 became 813. check_missing_3 found no empty text. inspect_4 showed short Reddit comments, for example "Calls on retards" (16 characters) and "Seeing lots of red in the ticker." (33 characters). describe_5 gave a mean length of 148 characters and a median of 80. In the box plot (../plots/Benjamin_Escuder_homework_plot_describe_5.png) the three classes have a similar box, all under 200 characters, with a long tail of outliers above. The longest post is 2753 characters and belongs to the neutral class. On this measure the three classes look close to each other.

**Sampling and the matrix.** sample_6 reduced the data to 200 posts with random_state=1, keeping 98 neutral, 78 negative and 24 positive. The positive class is left with 24 posts, which is small, and this is worth keeping in mind for the later steps. tokenize_7 found 7231 tokens, about 36 per post. binarize_8 one-hot encoded the three classes into a 200x3 matrix. I then built the document-term matrix with English stop words removed: 200 documents, 1759 terms, 2729 non-zero cells out of 351800, so 99.22% sparsity. After the last restart I rebuilt it with the same settings (dtm_26) and the numbers came out the same. The posts are short and most of them use different words, so the matrix is mostly empty.

**Frequent terms.** term_frequency_15 returned these top terms: just (27), like (25), trump (21), money (20), market (18), china (17), think (16), company (14), good (14). After removing stop words, the top of the list still holds general words such as "just", "like" and "think". "Good" is the one clear sentiment word in the list, while "trump", "china" and "market" point more to the topic of the post. dtm_heatmap_16 (../plots/Benjamin_Escuder_homework_plot_dtm_heatmap_16.png) shows only zeros. The tool takes the first 15 terms in vocabulary order, and those are numbers like "00", "000", "10k" and "116", which do not appear in the 15 posts shown. Sorting the terms by frequency instead of vocabulary order would give a more informative view.

**Filtering.** variance_filter_17 with threshold 0.01 kept 331 terms out of 1759 and removed 1428, so a large part of the vocabulary is near-constant across the sample. The highest variance term is "graphite" (0.40), followed by "like" (0.25) and "company" (0.24). pearson_18 on the positive class gave a highest correlation of 0.27, shared by six terms at the same value (tldr, green, follow, downtrend, potential, trend). With 24 positive posts in the sample, a tie like this can come from a small number of posts that contain all six words, so the result is to be taken lightly. spearman_19 on the negative class gave a highest value of 0.18, again with several terms tied (xi, americans, sure, treasuries, safe, great). Some of them, such as "safe" and "great", are not words usually associated with a negative tone.

**Correlation between terms.** feature_correlation_21 (../plots/Benjamin_Escuder_homework_plot_feature_correlation_21.png) shows a strongest pair of "graphite" and "northern" at 0.97, which matches the company name Northern Graphite. "Water" and "torrente" (0.51) form a similar pair. Other pairs fit a common theme: "portfolio" and "allocation" (0.76), "portfolio" and "list" (0.57), "money" and "think" (0.58), "like" and "company" (0.50). Most of the remaining cells sit near zero.

**Pattern mining and structure.** patterns_22 mined the positive class (24 posts, 299 terms, 259 kept after tf-idf) with FP-Growth and min_sup=3, and returned 3 patterns, all single words: [just] 4, [new] 3, [like] 3. patterns_27 on the neutral class (98 posts, 1031 terms, 855 kept) used min_sup=8 and also returned 3 single words: [just] 10, [like] 9, [day] 8. I used a different min_sup for each class because the class sizes differ: support 8 cannot be reached in a class of 24 posts, and support 3 in a class of 98 posts returns mostly rare terms. No returned pattern contains two words, so no pair of words reached the support level in either class. cosine_28 compared post 0 (neutral) and post 1 (positive) and returned 0.0, meaning the two posts share no term. reduce_29 ran PCA (../plots/Benjamin_Escuder_homework_plot_reduce_29.png), where the two components explain 8.56% and 6.11% of the variance. The plot shows one dense group of points near the origin with the three colors mixed together, and about ten outliers spread along the first dimension, most of them neutral.

**Conclusion.** Across these steps, the word counts of this sample did not show a clear separation between the three sentiment classes: the posts are short, the matrix is 99.22% sparse, the correlations with the labels stay below 0.3, and the strongest term pairs point to single companies rather than to a shared vocabulary. A next step could be to use the full 813 posts instead of 200, tf-idf weights instead of raw counts, and n-grams, since sentiment in this kind of text often sits in short phrases such as "not good" or "going up" rather than in single words.
